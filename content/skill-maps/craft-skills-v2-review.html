01Summary

  • 36 files: 13 skills and 23 scene recipes.
  • 3 new skills: craft-script-writer (a story written so every line can be drawn), craft-anchor (one staging picture per place and moment) and craft-previs (a grey 3D blockout in Blender).
  • 2 rewritten: the header script-to-video (6 steps became 8) and craft-director (scene types, each with its own camera grammar).
  • 8 almost unchanged: asset-designer, the 3 continuity skills and the 4 prompters. They were renamed with a craft- prefix and now say they never trigger on their own. The prompters also learned to use anchors and to write a feeling only as what shows.
  • Scene recipes: they moved from the director to the script writer and became writing recipes (their camera and video-model sections are gone). 9 are new.
  • Audio dropped: v2 no longer takes audio as a starting point (Idea, Brief, Script, Broken script). Our studio's script-to-video does, and it works.
  • Where the studio is: it runs v1-based versions. None of the 3 new skills is in. Our director is a mix of the studio's own and v1's.
  • Biggest wins for us: the anchor (H3 Chunk already has an "anchor" slot), the director's scene grammar, the script writer's object and physics rules, and the clip packing rules.
  • Prompting: the studio is better at how each model reads a prompt. Craft v2 is better at what goes into the prompt and how references and sound are wired. They work best together.
  • 16 decisions are listed at the end, each with a reason and my recommendation.

03What changed, skill by skill

script-to-video (the header)

v1: 1 Input → 2 Setup → 3 Models → 4 Assets → 5 Direct → 6 Generate
v2: 1 Input → 2 Setup → 3 Story → 4 Assets → 5 Plan → 6 Clips → 7 Anchors → 8 Generate
  1. Input: Idea, Brief, Script, Broken script. Audio is gone, and with it the transcript, the runtime read from the audio, the per-line dialogue clips and the original sound under the cut. When the message already carries the material, its kind is read from it and nothing is asked.
  2. Models decide more: the video model now picks the prompter and which continuity methods are offered. MiniMax H3 gets Board and Recall only: Chain is left out because H3 can't extend a clip. (v1 said the same: "on MiniMax, Chain becomes Board".)
  3. Setup questions:
    • The four questions: Convert into (Video, Images, Detailed script); Aspect ratio (16:9 default); Resolution (the model's own list, 720p default); Continuity (Chain by default where offered, else Board).
    • A question with only one option is not asked.
  4. Style picker: the search uses every art-style sub-category, so every style appears on the card.
  5. New Story step:
    • A full script is kept word for word.
    • Anything else gets one question ("How long should the story run?" 1, 3 (default), 5 or 8 min), then craft-script-writer writes the story.
    • "Detailed script" ends here, with the narrated story.
  6. Assets are made from the finished story, so the sheets cover everyone the story added.
  7. Plan: the director gets the story, the sheets, the style and the shape. Every scene is planned before any clip.
  8. New Clips step (v1's director did this):
    • One clip per scene. A scene longer than the model's longest clip splits at shot cuts into the fewest near-equal clips.
    • A flashback beat starts its own clip. A scene under 4 s joins the next one (the last scene joins the one before).
    • Clips are numbered across the whole piece, and every clip has the same fields. Its length is its shots added up, rounded up.
  9. New Anchors step (craft-anchor), for both video and pictures.
  10. Pictures: each shot is frozen at the peak of its action, with its anchors first among the references. The prompt opens "Keep the place, its light and where each figure stands from Image 1; the camera is this shot's."
  11. Continuity, simplified: a scene's first clip is a normal generation (under Chain and Board), and later clips use the method. Recall runs on every clip. v1's "join" types (continues, cut, new place) are gone.
  12. The prompter now gets the anchors. The style is the picked style word for word, then the era and world from the director's episode.
  13. Each continuity skill runs its own after-clip step.
  14. New state layout: /script/story, /director/episode, /director/scenes/<n>/beats|shots, /anchors/<k>, /s2v/clips/<n>.

Not in v2 but in our studio version: audio input, the final cut, the "Is this a film?" step 0, H3 Chunk's slots, the draft/final quality question.

craft-script-writer (new) and its 23 scene recipes

  • What it makes: one story, read aloud like an audio-series episode. Third person, past tense, a narrator who talks to the listener (asides, questions, teases), dialogue inline. No shots, camera, prompts or sheets.
  • Length: about 140 words a minute, within 10% of the runtime. An idea becomes about 1,200 words. Past about 10 minutes it splits into episodes, each ending on a hook.
  • What it keeps:
    • A full script keeps every event, line and name, with dialogue word for word.
    • A broken script keeps everything it has and repairs the rest. Nothing named is dropped or compressed.
  • Planning:
    • Whose eyes the story is told through, the biggest turn and the closing hook.
    • For each scene: who wants what, what blocks them, the flip (with the opposite feeling just before it) and one consequence beat after it.
    • The scene's kind, with that kind's recipe read in one round (preset_search "scene-<kind>", category scene-recipes).
    • What the listener knows that the lead doesn't, the plants (things set up early that matter later), and stakes sized to the moment.
  • Rules written for pictures:
    • Hands and carried objects are tracked. Before climbing, a door or a hug, the story says what happens to what they hold.
    • A look, once changed, holds. Every move and door is said, and each scene picks up where the last left off.
    • Small meaningful details get their own line.
    • Physics is real: weight, falls, rain, fire, glass, breathlessness, locked doors, travel time, size and fit, two hands for two things, clothes that slow you, marks that stay, light in the dark, how far sound carries.
    • People only know what they've seen or been told. Time and weather hold, and so does who is in the room.
    • Places keep their layout. Nobody uses a skill the story hasn't shown, and every surprising choice has a visible reason.
  • Prose rules:
    • Written for the ear.
    • Banned machine phrases ("a breath she didn't know she was holding"…) and stock fantasy names (Elara, Kael…).
    • No "not X but Y", no lists of three, no little morals.
    • Concrete, never abstract. Each person talks their own way, gestures and openings vary, and feelings take time.
  • Output: "Ep N - <title>" and plain paragraphs, posted as one fenced block and saved at /script/story, then a final checklist.
  • Scene recipes: each has the same seven parts (what it's about, the engine, staging, performance, the turn, the end, traps).

craft-director (rewritten)

  • Top down: episode → scenes → beats → shots, each level built only from the one above, saved as it goes. The story is locked; the plan only decides what is seen.
  • Episode:
    • The story in one line, and the arc of feelings scene by scene.
    • The leads, each with a tell: one small physical giveaway, shown as an insert whenever the feeling leaks.
    • The world: money, rank and power; the rival and the secret; the look of any power; one fixed description for everything that recurs.
    • The style, and one era: real light sources only, no neon unless it's a club.
    • An opening that hooks on the leads in motion and shows the event, never only its aftermath.
    • A throughline (opening image, closing image, one motif between them) and the hinge scenes.
  • 27 scene types: Meet, Friction, Pull, Surrender, Heat, Rejection, Humiliation, Jealousy, Protection, Confession, Betrayal, False hope, Decision, Comeback, Awakening, Bond, Reunion, Intimacy, Longing, Threat, Trial, Revelation, Claim, Reversal, Triumph, Status, Backstory.
    • Each fixes a camera height, a frame, a light, a camera style and one exception shot.
    • Tiers: T1 is lighter, T2 as written, T3 pushed hardest.
    • Each scene sets a feeling in → out, its tone (palette, one saturated hue), its pace (holds of 1.5–5 s by kind) and its build (wide → medium → close).
  • Echoes: a payoff scene reuses the setups of the scene it pays off, with the outcome reversed.
  • Beats: sequence, montage, flashback, insert or intercut, each with one instruction line.
  • Shots:
    • A table turns each kind of line into a picture.
    • Dialogue rules: the speaker is shown mid-line, a line that lands cuts to the listener, a line over 3 s splits, two people go over the shoulder with matched eyelines.
    • Every shot names its picture, everyone in frame (by sheet name, where they are, which way they face), the motion, the move, the angle, the size and the length (never under 1.5 s: models merge shorter shots).
    • Camera limits: never more than 2 locked shots in a row, and a hold past 4 s drifts. Romantic scenes stay calm. No dutch angle in tender scenes. No descending onto a character.
    • Pictures: no readable text in any picture, at least 3 angles a scene, visible hard cuts, and nothing shown that the story hasn't reached.
  • A check of each scene before saving.

v2 no longer has these v1 parts. Our studio director still contains most of them:

  • The "This scene is shot as ___" idea sentence.
  • Want / beats as verbs / turn / whose eyes / withheld per scene. Part of this moved to the script writer.
  • Lens and aperture ranges, rigs, frame rate and shutter, and sound notes.
  • Cut reasons and "Cut to (reason)", and held takes written in timed slots.
  • Clip sizes per model, timing from a transcript, and the voiceover field.
  • Writing it as a book, the story and image formats, and the "simplify before finishing" step.

craft-anchor (new)

  • An anchor is one 16:9 still of a staging state (a place at one moment with everyone in it where they stand, and its light). It is never shown on screen. Every clip in that state takes it as its first reference, so nobody vanishes, swaps or drifts.
  • A new state starts when someone enters, leaves or moves to a new spot, the place changes, the light changes, or a look changes and carries on into a later clip.
  • Anchored when: 2 or more people or creatures are in the state, or its shots run across 2 or more clips. Not anchored: chases and journeys, montages and flashes, inserts, empty places (the location sheet is the anchor) and one person alone in one clip.
  • Camera: one per place, at eye height, a normal lens, placed to see everyone, and kept for every later state of that place.
  • Prompt:
    • Keep → Subject → Style, 80–150 words (200 with 4 or more figures).
    • Positive words only, one choice each, no digits except the image numbers.
    • Everyone is "the figure in Image N", and a creature's size is stated against a person.
    • Separate sentences for touching people, a changed look, a place with no sheet, and a style that isn't realistic.
  • Making them:
    • All in one go on Seedream 5 Pro at 2K, always 16:9.
    • References go people first, then creatures and props, the location sheet last.
    • A later state of a place is a new picture from the same camera, never an edit of the earlier one.
    • A CSV (anchor, scene and state, clips, link) is posted, and each anchor's positions are saved in set terms.

craft-previs (new)

  • What it makes: a grey 3D blockout in Blender 5.2, run headless in a GPU sandbox. It is never shown in the film; it fixes who stands where, who moves when, and what each camera sees.
  • Step 1, build:
    • One JSON per scene: the room or ground in metres, set pieces at real sizes, the cast with heights and colours, and keys for walking, facing, pose and entering or leaving, on the scene's clock.
    • It renders an overview video with names over heads, fixes the WARN lines, posts a CSV and asks "Is the blocking right?" (Approve or Change something).
  • Step 2, cameras:
    • One camera per planned shot (size, side, angle, lens; moves mapped from the plan).
    • It renders grey clips per clip and a sheet of each shot's first frame.
    • It fixes every CHECK line that says someone planned is MISSING or someone unplanned is in frame, then posts a CSV.
  • The whole previs.py (about 600 lines) is inside the skill, saved word for word.
  • v2's header never calls it: it is a standalone tool.

Continuity (3 skills)

The bodies are the same as v1, renamed, and they now say they never trigger on their own. What changed is around them: the header now decides continuity by a scene's first clip versus its later clips, and offers no Chain on H3.

Prompters (4 skills)

  • A new anchor input, wired first among the pictures with its own line ("a reference for space and positions, not a frame to copy; faces and looks come from the sheets").
  • Anchors come second in the order of what to keep when there are too many references.
  • A feeling goes in only as its visible tell ("knuckles whiten on the glass"). A feeling with no tell is dropped.
  • Wan 3: a source clip is cut to its last 15 s (was 14), and the new clip is 15 s at most.

craft-asset-designer

Same body as v1. Our port already does all of it, with names, looks, palettes and relatives kept in NeuralCore. The only differences:

  • The craft's final CSV has a "CDN link" column whose asset_id: handles become links on Download and Copy.
  • The craft makes sheets on Seedream; ours use any picture workflow that takes references.

07Our prompting compared with craft v2

The studio today

  • System rules:
    • One shot is one action, and prompts are in English while spoken words keep the user's language.
    • No settings or @tags inside a prompt. Every prompt is a concrete, complete shot, and a person's description is repeated word for word across shots.
    • Lengths come from the action and the speech rate (2.5 words a second).
  • A general structure for workflows with no guide: SCENE CONTEXT, REFERENCES, ACTION, CAMERA, LIGHT AND LOOK, AUDIO.
  • 8 prompt guides (added today): MiniMax H3, LTX, Wan, FLUX.2, FLUX.3 Klein, HunyuanImage, Krea 2, Qwen Image. Each has a target length and a word limit, and a prompt over the limit is sent back.
  • Each workflow's hints: H3 Chunk has 10.
  • Structured camera fields: shot size, angle, move and end state are written into words. The "place" field chains shots and checks continuity.
  • Other helpers:
    • A preset library with clashes.
    • "look deep" gives lists of what to keep and what to avoid.
    • Hindi respelling, cleaning the voice before lip-sync, and a "check" note for each review.

Craft v2

  • The director decides what happens; the prompter only places it. "With no written scene, write only what the user said: do not add camera, light or acting they did not ask for."
  • Blocks per model: GOAL, ASSETS, SCENE, OPENING, SHOTS, STYLE, END STATE, LOCKS (Wan has its own five sections).
  • Wiring: one role line per reference, with one subject and one job each. Never cite a tag that isn't wired, and never tag someone in a shot they're not in.
  • Too many references: keep them in a fixed order (named ones, anchors, speakers, the place, others, props) and say what was dropped.
  • Sound: each character's voice sample is bound on their line. Recorded lines are bound twice, plus an "as-is" lock, and clips are all or nothing.
  • Continuity in the prompt: text, staging grid, reference grid, first frame, anchor and fresh start, which can be stacked.
  • A report after each call: what was made, what was dropped, the settings locked, and any gaps.
  • Templates for sheets and anchors. A feeling goes in only as its visible tell.

Where they clash

  • Several shots in one clip:
    • Craft packs a whole scene into one clip, every shot with its own framing and lens, locked to "N cuts exactly, only where stated".
    • Our H3 guide says a new framing in a beat is a hard cut: write one only when a cut was asked for, then say "Cut to".
    • They agree if the planned cuts count as asked for: each planned shot after the first starts "Cut to", with a keyframe per shot (H3 Chunk 311–316) and shot cut frames for exact timing. One line in our guide would settle it.
  • Tag names: craft writes @Image1 and @Audio1 (Frameo's names). In H3 Chunk, pictures are <Picture 1> (the anchor), <Picture 2> and on, and people are "character 1". This is a name swap only, and the rules stay the same.
  • Filling in versus fidelity: inside a skill run, follow craft (write only what the plan says). Outside a run, keep our complete-shot rule.

Verdict: neither replaces the other. The studio is better at how each model reads a prompt. Craft v2 is better at what goes into it and how references and sound are wired. Best: keep our guides, port the craft H3 prompter as a studio craft skill used inside script-to-video (all its text kept, with the tag names swapped), and add the one-line fix for cuts.

08Decisions before changing anything

Each needs your answer because it either adds to v2 or changes what our studio does today. Answered on 4 November: all sixteen as recommended.

D1Audio as a starting point

v2 removed it. Ours works (transcribe with speakers, slices for each clip, the original sound under the cut), and you asked about script plus audio.

Recommend keep it as a fifth Input option and leave the rest of v2 as written. Dropping it removes a working feature.

D2The final cut

v2 stops after the clips. Our last step puts them together.

Recommend keep it as the last step. Without it there is no finished film.

D3Step 0 "Is this a film?"

Added today so one-off clips and statements don't start the whole skill. It isn't in v2.

Recommend keep it. It belongs to our default-skill rule, not to the craft.

D4Chain on H3

v2 (and v1) give H3 only Board and Recall. Today ours offers Chain from the last frame, as the default.

Recommend follow v2: Board by default, with Recall offered. Bring Chain back when a true extension is wired. Starting on a last frame drops the sheets, so faces drift.

D5The director: replace or merge

v2's director is a different method. Ours mixes the studio's own steps with v1's craft, and the two grammars conflict: v1 says never move the camera by default and about 6 shots per 30 s, while v2 says no more than 2 locked shots in a row and 1.5–2.5 s holds for action.

Recommend v2's text whole as the base, plus only our app steps: show the plan as a table and ask, waves of 12 steps with save_to and check, reviews and retakes, then the cut. Drop v1's extras (idea sentence, lens ranges, cut reasons, book writing) unless you want them kept.
