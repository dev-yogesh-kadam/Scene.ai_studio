# Decision / dilemma / the choice
Use when one character, mostly alone, faces two options and chooses: sign or refuse, leave or stay, tell the truth, take the deal, use the power. Mix with `suspense` when time is short, `grief` for a choice forced by loss, `romance` for choosing a person.

## What the scene is really about
- Two goods, or two harms. Right against wrong is no dilemma; the cost on both sides holds the scene.
- The body chooses before the mind admits it: a hand that moves, a foot that turns.
- It changes who the character is. After it, there's no way back.

## Pick the engine
- **Two objects:** the options are physical things in the room (the ticket and the ring, the pen and the phone); the scene is the hand between them.
- **The threshold:** a door, a car, a gate; going through is the choice.
- **The clock:** a deadline in the world (the train, the call, footsteps at the door) forces it.
- **The voice:** someone's line (a message, a remembered word) plays in the character's head while they hold still.
- **The reflection:** they face themselves (a mirror, a dark window, an old photo) and decide who to be.
- **The non-choice:** they refuse to pick, and that is the choice.

## Staging and blocking
- Split the space: one option on each side, the character between them.
- Give the choice a physical act: sign, tear, pick up, put down, walk, open, close.
- Let them move toward one side, stop, and turn back once before the real choice.
- Use the story's own objects, never an invented symbol.
- Keep others away or far off; the choice belongs to one person.

## Performance
- Thought shows as stillness broken by small moves: a breath, a hand lifting and falling, eyes going to one option and away.
- The choice itself is one honest act at full size.
- Relief or grief after it, never triumph; even the right choice costs.

## The turn
- The act itself: the pen moving, the door opening, the phone set down.
- Put a near-choice the other way just before it.

## After the turn / the end
- One beat of what it costs: the option left behind, seen once more.
- End on the person moving into what they chose.

## Traps
- An easy choice dressed up as a hard one.
- The choice said aloud ("I have to decide").
- A symbolic device (a coin flip, a fork in the road) where an object of the story would do.
- Turning to other people during the choice.
- Skipping the cost.
