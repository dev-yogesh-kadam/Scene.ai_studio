# Romance / intimacy
First meeting, flirtation, confession, kiss, intimacy, farewell, drift. Mix with `conversation` when it is mostly talk, `grief` for a loss, `reveal` for a recognition.

## What the scene is really about
- Distance is the story. The gap between two bodies shrinks across the scene, or grows in a scene about drifting apart.
- Every romance beat is a risk: someone shows more than is safe. Find who risks first and what it costs.
- The feeling lives in what they don't do: the touch held back, the line not said, the look that slides away.

## Pick the engine
- **The near-miss:** looks that nearly meet and slide away; the scene is the waiting, not the touch.
- **The pursuit:** one reaches, the other retreats, then the retreat stops.
- **The task between them:** they do something together (a dance, a meal, mending something) and the closeness grows out of the task.
- **The touch that opens:** contact comes first and the scene celebrates it, rather than building toward it.
- **The farewell given:** the last look or the last touch is given in full and is still not enough.
- **The drift:** joy that drains with no cause, played on two faces.

## Staging and blocking
- Begin with something between them: a table, a counter, a crowd, a doorway, a third person. The scene is about getting past it.
- Let them come together on their own feet; don't build a device to put them together (a reflection, a note, a lucky collision).
- Give the closing of distance stops: a step, a pause, half a step back, then the last step.
- A space can change meaning: the place of the first touch can become the place of the goodbye.
- A mirror figure can help: a second pair nearby, or a witness, whose small echo shows what the couple can't say.

## Performance
- Play verbs: to tease, to test, to dare, to retreat, to give in. Not "romantic".
- The body says it before the mouth: leaning in while saying no, a hand that stops halfway.
- Flirtation is a game with rules; the turn is the moment one of them stops playing.
- Confession works best half-said, or said about something else. If the words must be said, say them plainly once and stay on the listener.

## The turn
- The moment one of them closes the last distance, or stops closing it.
- Keep the touch for the last possible moment; don't let an earlier beat spend it. If it opens the scene, make that the choice.
- Before the turn, put the opposite: a retreat, a joke, a near-goodbye, so the touch reverses something.

## After the turn / the end
- One consequence beat: the first look after the kiss, the step back, the decision to leave anyway.
- A farewell can end on the one who stays, or the one who goes looking back. End on a face, not an object.
- The ending can carry doom or doubt, not only warmth. Let the ending hold something larger than them (sky, sea, the empty road).
- In drift, stay close on the faces. Pulling far back shrinks the thing the scene lives on.

## Traps
- Subtext said aloud ("I think I'm falling for you" when a look already said it).
- Gimmick props or staged devices that explain the feeling or arrange the meeting; let them simply reach each other.
- Contradicting your own withheld touch (a hand taken in the second beat when the story saves it for the last).
- Withholding by habit: a last look that hurts because it is given, hidden instead.
- Inventing a cause for what should stay ambiguous; drift has no trigger.
- One-sided recognition: only one face shown when the scene belongs to both.
- A warm, sealed ending when the scene carries a coming loss.
