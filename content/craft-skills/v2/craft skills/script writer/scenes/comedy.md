# Comedy
Use for physical, situational, deadpan, cringe or verbal gags. Mix with `fight` for action comedy, `confrontation` for cringe and status, `romance` for a romcom beat.

## What the scene is really about
- A gap between what someone believes and what is true, and how long they can keep believing it. The laugh is the gap closing.
- Dignity under pressure. The harder someone guards their status, the funnier its loss.
- The audience is usually one step ahead; timing decides when the character catches up.

## Pick the engine
- **Oblivious hero:** the audience knows more, and the joke is what he doesn't see. Travel with him in one take.
- **Misread cause:** he blames the wrong thing for what is happening, and keeps testing it.
- **Escalation:** each step makes it worse. Play it to its full physical end (the tip, the fall, the narrow escape); don't stop at the reveal.
- **Anticlimax:** a huge build, then an abrupt payoff. Speed is the joke.
- **Absurd logic wins:** the premise's impossible claim comes true by accident (a lucky chain of events) rather than simply failing.
- **Deadpan / cringe:** to the person causing it, nothing is wrong; the pause after the wrong thing is the joke, and the bystanders carry it.

## Staging and blocking
- Keep cause and effect together: action and reaction in the same moment, never told apart.
- Stage gags in depth; the background can carry what the hero doesn't notice.
- Use what enters, what leaves, what was just out of sight. Someone walks toward a thing, sees it, and walks straight back out the way they came.
- A gag usually has one clear geometry where it reads. Find it and keep it.
- Start the hero at a disadvantage (wrong clothes, wrong tool, too small); the fix he improvises is the gag.
- Use what the place already holds (a door, a ladder, a wet floor), not a prop brought in to be funny.
- Two beats set a pattern, the third breaks it. Vary the third; never repeat it.

## Performance
- Play it straight. The character means it; nobody winks at the audience.
- A still face while the world breaks around it is the strongest reaction there is.
- One honest reaction, sized right: a slow look down, a double take, a long blink, a beat of nothing, then the bolt.
- Let the bump hurt a little. An invulnerable clown isn't funny; pain makes him human.
- Bystanders are alive: the one sceptic, the clerk who saw everything, the dog.

## The turn
- The gap closes: he sees it, it falls, the claim comes true, the silence breaks. Put peak confidence just before it (the eyebrow, the swagger, "watch this").
- Deliver it at full size. A disaster promised is a disaster delivered.
- A topper may follow from a different direction (the sceptic's line, a second collapse). One only.

## After the turn / the end
- Hold on the reaction one beat, then move on; a held explanation kills the joke.
- When the topper is a line or a reveal, end on it and the face; no physical tail after the laugh. Physical gags still play to their full end.
- One sceptic's deadpan look can close it.
- Get out fast. Leaving the scene is part of the timing.

## Traps
- Breaking away at the payoff, or stopping mid-gag to explain it.
- Scaling the payoff down: a small slip where the premise promised a disaster, a fail where it promised the absurd success.
- Explaining the joke: a long aftermath, a physical tail after a verbal topper, an object left floating after the laugh.
- Holding the reaction past one beat because "hold on the reaction" was read as a rule.
- A constructed device (a reflection, a sign gag) where continuous background business would do.
- Everyone mugging. The characters must believe it.
