# Humiliation / comeback / reversal
Use for public shame and its payoff: the slap at the engagement party, divorce papers before the family, the servant mocked at the gate, then the return that turns it around (a new look, a new rank, a hidden identity out, the shamer brought low). Usually two scenes, the shame planted early and the payoff later. Mix with `confrontation` for the exchange, `introduction` for the return, `status` for the display, `triumph` for the release.

## What the scene is really about
- Status in front of witnesses. The shame needs an audience, and the payoff needs the same one; shame in private is only cruelty.
- The pair is one scene split in two. The comeback lands only as hard as the humiliation hurt.
- The comeback is not revenge for its own sake: the world finally sees who the hero is. The shamer's face is the prize.

## Pick the engine
- **Public shaming:** the hero lowered before a crowd (a slap, a drink thrown, a ring handed back, a name struck off), the witnesses choosing to laugh or look away.
- **Quiet contempt:** no blow at all; a seat not offered, a plate served last, a name forgotten on purpose.
- **The return:** the one looked down on arrives changed; the room recognises them in stages.
- **Identity out:** the hero was always more (the heir, the owner, the master), and one act or one person exposes it.
- **Tables turned:** the shamer now needs something from the hero (the contract, the cure, the vote) and has to ask for it.
- **Mirror payoff:** the comeback restages the humiliation (same place, same setup), roles swapped.

## Staging and blocking
- Map the humiliation: the shamer above (on the stairs, at the head of the table, standing while the hero kneels), the witnesses in a ring, the exits closed.
- Plant what the payoff will reuse: the place, the key witnesses, the object (the thrown ring, the torn invitation), the shamer's exact gesture.
- The hero takes the blow with one physical answer: stays standing, picks up the ring, walks out slowly. Never a speech.
- At the return, the same room changes its blocking: the crowd parts, people rise, the shamer is now the one seated or below.
- Show the return through others first: heads turning, a glass stopping halfway, a whisper travelling across the room.
- The shamer's fall is a body: a smile held a second too long, a step back, the drink set down, a knee bending.

## Performance
- The shamer enjoys it and plays to the room; contempt is casual, rarely shouted.
- The hero's answer is smaller than the insult: stillness, a swallow, a hand closing. What they hold back charges the payoff.
- At the return the hero is calm and specific; the change shows in posture, eyeline and whom they look at (not the shamer, at first).
- Witnesses switch sides in the body: the ones who laughed now study the floor.

## The turn
- In the humiliation: the moment the hero stops defending themselves. In the comeback: the moment the shamer understands who is standing there.
- Put the shamer at peak confidence just before (a toast, a joke, a last jab).
- Let someone else trigger it where possible: a guard bows, the chairman stands, the call comes through.

## After the turn / the end
- One beat of the new order: the shamer below, the room silent, the hero taking the seat or leaving.
- One quiet line or none; never a revenge speech.
- The hero can turn away without looking back while the shamer's face stays with us.

## Traps
- A comeback that doesn't reuse the humiliation's place, witnesses or setup, so nothing reverses.
- A hero who argues back during the humiliation, spending the payoff early.
- A revenge speech where the shamer's face would do.
- A cartoon shamer: sneering every line, no charm, no reason the room followed them.
- The return shown from behind or in the dark; give the face in full.
- A payoff smaller than the shame: an awkward silence where the setup earned a fall.
