# Trial / ceremony / formal reckoning
Use when a judgment or a rite happens before watchers: a court, a boardroom vote, a family council, a wedding, an engagement, a coronation, a sect's assessment. Mix with `confrontation` for the exchange, `reveal` for the evidence that flips it, `humiliation` when the result shames, `performance` for a test of skill.

## What the scene is really about
- A ritual with rules everyone knows, and one person whose fate it decides.
- The form is the pressure: they can't leave, can't shout, must wait their turn.
- The audience waits for the verdict, the vow or the result, and expects something to break the form.

## Pick the engine
- **The verdict:** evidence, argument, then the judgment, landing on the face of the judged.
- **The interrupted rite:** the wedding, the signing or the vow stopped at the worst moment (an objection, the door opening, the bride walking out).
- **The vote:** hands rise one by one and the count turns.
- **The assessment:** the young one tested before elders (a power gauge, an exam, an audition), the result shown to everyone.
- **The family council:** relatives at a long table deciding a life (a marriage, an inheritance, a banishment).
- **The coronation:** rank given or taken in public, and who bows.

## Staging and blocking
- Ritual space has a shape (the aisle, the dais, the long table, the bench). Use it; it decides who is above.
- Isolate the judged one: alone in the aisle, at the far end of the table, in the centre of a circle.
- Watchers are a mass with a few key faces: the one who wants the fall, the one who hopes, the one who will speak.
- Every rite runs its props in a set order (rings, garlands, contract, seal, gavel). Know the order; the break interrupts it.
- Give the judged one small private gestures against the formality: a hand finding a sleeve, a glance at one face.

## Performance
- The judged one holds still against the pressure; the tells are breath, hands and a face that refuses to move.
- The officiant, judge or elder is calm and procedural; the cruelty is in the procedure.
- One watcher breaks the room's silence at the turn.

## The turn
- The result, the refused vow, the objection, the evidence produced, the vote that flips.
- Put the opposite just before it: the rite running smoothly, the verdict all but certain.

## After the turn / the end
- One beat of the room absorbing it, then the order rearranges: who stands, who leaves, who bows.
- End on the judged one and what the result opens onto.

## Traps
- Moving on before the verdict lands on the face.
- A rite with no visible form, so nothing can break it.
- Every watcher reacting the same way.
- A speech in place of the event that flips the result.
- Ending on the gavel or the ring instead of the person.
