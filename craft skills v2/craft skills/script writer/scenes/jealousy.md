# Jealousy / rival / triangle
Use when a third person closes in on one of a pair: the ex at the party, the other woman at the office, the rival suitor, the friend who wants what you have. Mix with `romance` for the pair, `confrontation` once it is said aloud, `suspense` when one of them is watching unseen.

## What the scene is really about
- Three points, one distance. The scene is the gap between the two who belong together, and the rival standing in it.
- Jealousy is watching: one sees something, reads it (often wrong), and can't say it.
- The rival wants something real (the person, the status, revenge). A rival with no want is a prop.

## Pick the engine
- **The watcher:** one sees the other with the rival across a room; the scene is what they see and what it does to their face.
- **The intrusion:** the rival walks into the pair's moment (a dinner, a doorway, a dance) and takes the space.
- **The provocation:** one uses the rival to sting the other: a laugh too loud, a hand on an arm, a dance held too long.
- **The misread:** an innocent moment seen the wrong way; the audience sees both what happened and what was seen.
- **The rival's move:** a quiet, calculated act (a gift, a whisper, a shared past brought up) the pair can't answer.
- **Claimed back:** the one in the middle chooses, in front of the rival.

## Staging and blocking
- Triangulate: put the rival physically between the two, or put the jealous one outside, watching the other pair.
- Give the watcher a vantage: across the room, through a doorway or glass, from a balcony or a car.
- Territory is small and exact: who sits beside whom, whose arm is taken, who gets the first dance, whose glass is filled.
- The rival touches what the jealous one thinks is theirs: the partner's sleeve, the chair, the space beside them.
- Give the jealous one something to do with their hands: a glass held too tight, a phone turned face down, a napkin folded and refolded.

## Performance
- Jealousy hides behind composure: a smile that never reaches the eyes, a laugh a beat late, a question too casual.
- The rival is charming and at ease; the danger is how easily they fit.
- The one in the middle is either oblivious or enjoying it. Decide which and show it.
- Size it to the person: a proud one goes cold and polite, an open one walks out.

## The turn
- The jealous one breaks cover: leaves, interrupts, says it, or does the thing they'll regret.
- Or the one in the middle looks past the rival, straight at the watcher.
- Put a moment of peace just before it (the rival leaving, the partner coming back) so the sting reverses it.

## After the turn / the end
- One beat of consequence: the rival's small smile, the partner's confusion, the watcher alone with what they did.
- A beaten rival shows it in the body: the hand dropped, the chair left empty.

## Traps
- The jealous one explaining what they feel.
- A faceless rival with no want or manner.
- Everyone together, so nobody is left out of anything.
- A misread whose truth the audience never sees.
- A shouting match where one look would land harder.
