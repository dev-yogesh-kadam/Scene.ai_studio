# Reveal / twist / recognition
Use when someone (or the audience) learns what was true all along: a twist, a discovery, a recognition, a realisation. Mix with `suspense` for discovery under threat, `grief` when the truth is a loss, `romance` for recognition between people.

## What the scene is really about
- Not the fact; the change in a person as they learn it. The reveal is a face rearranging everything it knew.
- What was there all along. A fair reveal makes the past re-read itself; a cheat adds something new.
- Decide who learns first: character and audience together, the audience first (we wait for him to catch up), or the character first (we read it on his face before we see what he saw).

## Pick the engine
- **Discovery:** he walks into a space and finds it; the camera follows him in, and what was out of frame comes into it.
- **Reaction first:** hold on the face that sees and keep the cause back as long as the face can carry it, often to the final frame.
- **Re-read:** a plant returns and means something else; a quick run of earlier moments, now understood differently.
- **Recognition:** two people realise who the other is through a physical act (a touch, a habit, a gesture), not a line.
- **Pull-back:** start tight on something ordinary and widen until the frame shows where it really is.
- **False hope:** what reads as rescue or relief is the trap (the light is the fire), so the reveal flips relief into dread or back.

## Staging and blocking
- Plant it in plain sight early, as physical evidence the audience could have read (a breath that doesn't fog, a name on a wall, a shape on the horizon), placed while attention is elsewhere.
- Plants are things the place would naturally hold, not props made to be clues. Behaviour plants best: nobody answers him, she never touches the cup.
- Hide by staging, not darkness: a shoulder, a doorframe, a crowd, depth of focus, the frame edge. Clear geography makes the reveal legible.
- Let a character walk us in, so the reveal is one move through the same space and what was there all along becomes visible.
- Recognition is two-sided: stage both faces where they read, alternate them, and give the last close-up to the one who is recognised.

## Camera and cutting
- The frame that hid it is the frame that shows it. One move through the space beats a cut to an insert.
- Reaction before cause: the face, then the thing. The face tells us how big it is.
- A re-read is a burst of earlier frames, each a fraction of a second, cut against the character's stillness, then back to the face.
- A dolly zoom or rack focus only when perception itself breaks; don't stack devices on a reveal that works plain.
- Give the revealed thing its full size: a body, a creature, a ruin, a face, all of it, in a clear frame.
- At a realisation, sound usually adds (earlier voices return, a theme enters) rather than going silent.

## Performance
- Play the opposite before it: certainty, relief, routine. The reveal reverses something.
- Understanding arrives in stages on the face: noticing, refusing, accepting.
- Size the reaction to what it means for this person: a step back, a laugh that dies, the body folding, a long stillness. Not a stock gasp.
- The other side is alive too: the liar who stops limping, the recognised one who doesn't move.

## The turn
- Land it at the peak, around the last fifth of the clip, never midway with idle time after.
- Prefer a turn someone causes (a ring rolls, she turns round, the door opens) over a private thought.
- Withhold the cause, never the event: once it turns, show it in full.

## After the turn / the end
- Land on the revealed thing or the face that understands it. After that, only a consequence beat if the scene needs one (he runs, the trap shuts, the liar drives away).
- Pay off every plant in that beat; don't introduce new ones.
- End on what the truth opens onto, not an emblem that sums it up.

## For the video model
- Camera-move reveals render well: a slow push past a doorframe, a pull-back, a pan. One move per shot, with the end frame stating exactly what is now visible.
- The model won't keep a secret on its own: say what is hidden and where (behind the pillar, out of frame left), or it shows it in frame one. Split the hiding and the showing into two shots if needed.
- Faces render the reaction well; name the physical change (colour drains, jaw slackens, eyes drop to the hand).
- Plants must be big and simple to survive: a shape, a colour, a mark, not small text or fine detail.
- A flashback burst is several short clips cut together; one clip can't flash back.
- In-shot transformations (a face changing, a room becoming another) warp; cut between the two states instead.

## Traps
- Revealing mid-clip, then idling for several seconds.
- Over-neat symbolic objects that state the twist (a label, a reflection that explains it).
- A plant never paid off, or a payoff never planted.
- Withholding the event itself instead of its cause, so the clip has no climax.
- A partial reveal (an eye at the window) when the scene promised the whole thing.
- Recognition played from one side only.
