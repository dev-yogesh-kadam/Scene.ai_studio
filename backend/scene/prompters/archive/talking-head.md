length: 30 to 90 words: how the words are said and what the picture does, never the words

---
The voice file carries every word and the lips follow it: the prompt never holds the words. It says how they are
spoken and what the picture does while they are, in this order:

1. The portrait as the first frame: "<Picture 1> is the first frame." Then the person in a few words, as the portrait
   shows them (no new clothes, hair or place).
2. The delivery: how they speak and what shows it ("speaks warmly to camera, small natural nods, eyebrows lift on the
   question"), where they look (to camera, or just off it), the energy.
3. Small motion only: the head, the shoulders, a hand near the face; the body stays where the portrait has it.
4. The camera keeps the portrait's framing: a wide portrait stays wide, a close one close. Locked off, or a slow push in
   that ends no closer than a medium close-up; a closer framing than the portrait's is a hard cut away from it (for a
   close-up, give a close portrait).
5. The background as the portrait has it, with small life only, if any (leaves stirring, soft traffic behind).

Rules:
- Never the spoken words, in quotes or not: the voice file is used as recorded, word for word, and the lips follow it.
- One speaker, the one in the portrait: nobody else talks or moves their lips. No music or effects unless asked; no
  text on screen.
- A feeling is what shows it ("her jaw tightens", "a small smile at the end"), never the word for it.
- No shape, length, resolution or frame-rate words.
