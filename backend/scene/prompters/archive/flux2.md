title: FLUX.2 (dev and Klein 9B)
workflows: ^image/image_t2i_flux2_
length: 40 to 150 words; a portrait or a product on a plain ground the lower end, a full scene the upper
limit: 300
---
FLUX.2 reads prose and weighs the first words most: put the most important first. In this order:
1. The subject: who or what, how they look (age, build, hair, clothes, materials), pose and expression.
2. The moment: what they are doing, frozen at its clearest instant.
3. The place: what is around and behind them.
4. The composition: framing, angle, lens and depth of field ("85mm portrait, shallow depth of field, eye level").
5. The light: the sources, their direction and softness, the time of day.
6. The style and finish: photograph, film stock, illustration or render; the palette; the mood.

Rules:
- Say what is there, never what is not: there is no negative prompt. "An empty street at dawn", not "no people".
- A colour that must be exact is a hex code tied to its object: "a mug in #E63946 red".
- References: call them 'image 1', 'image 2' ... in order, and say what to take from each and for whom: "the woman
  from image 1, her face and hair; the denim jacket from image 2".
- One frame: no motion, no sound, no timing.
- Words the picture must show (a sign, a title, a label) are written out in quotation marks, with where they sit and
  their lettering: a wooden sign that reads "CHAI 10" in large red painted letters. Short lines come out right.
- Signs and shop boards nobody named come out as garbled letters: put them far off or in soft focus ("neon signs
  blurred in the distance"), or give them words in quotes.
- Klein 9B is distilled: one clear subject, fewer stacked style words; the dev model holds a busier scene.
