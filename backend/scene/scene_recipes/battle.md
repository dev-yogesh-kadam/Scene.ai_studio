# Battle / war / mass action
Use when many fight at once: armies clashing, a siege, a sect war, a raid on a village, a beast loose in a crowd, a city falling. Mix with `fight` for a one-on-one inside it, `chase` for the rout, `awakening` for a power that turns it, `grief` for the aftermath, `triumph` for the win.

## What the scene is really about
- One person inside chaos. The battle is the world; the scene is what it does to someone we know.
- Scale and cost: how many, how big, and what is lost.
- The tide: who is winning, and the moment it changes.

## Pick the engine
- **The soldier's eye:** stay with one fighter through it; everything else is noise around them.
- **The commander's view:** the shape of the battle from above, then down into its cost.
- **The line breaks:** a wall, a gate or a shield line holds, holds, and gives.
- **The champion:** the battle opens a space around one duel.
- **The arrival:** reinforcements, a beast or a power enters and turns the tide.
- **The field after:** one person walks what is left.

## Staging and blocking
- Set the map first: who is where, what they fight for (the gate, the hill, the village), which way is forward.
- Strict screen direction: one side always enters from the left, the other from the right.
- Keep the hero findable: a colour, a banner, a weapon, a silhouette nobody else has.
- Layer every moment: the hero in front, the clash around them, the scale in the distance.
- Cost in specifics: a friend falls, a house burns, a child runs through the smoke.

## Performance
- Fear before the charge, fatigue during it, shock after.
- The hero has an aim inside the battle (reach someone, hold the gate, find the leader); the battle keeps blocking it.
- Commanders stay calm; soldiers show fear.

## The turn
- The tide changes: the line breaks, the champion falls, the beast arrives, the hero's power shows.
- Put the losing side at the edge of winning just before it.

## After the turn / the end
- The cost seen: the field, the fallen friend, the burning home.
- End on the hero, standing or not.

## Traps
- A battle with nobody to follow.
- No map, so nobody knows who is winning.
- Only the scale, never a person, so nothing is felt.
- Anonymous bodies in a blur.
- No cost.
- An aftermath held too long, or skipped.
