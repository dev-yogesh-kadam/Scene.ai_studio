# Status / wealth / power on display
Use when money, rank or power is the point: the family's mansion, the CEO walking the floor, a car pulling up, an order that moves a room, the servant's view of the masters. Mix with `introduction` for a powerful newcomer, `humiliation` when status is used against someone, `confrontation` when two powers meet.

## What the scene is really about
- Power seen through other people. Rank is what everyone else does: stand, step aside, fall silent, hurry.
- The gap: what the powerful take for granted and the powerless can't touch. The audience should feel it.
- Display has a purpose: to impress, to intimidate, to remind someone of their place. Know which.

## Pick the engine
- **The walk:** the powerful one moves through their world and everyone adjusts around them.
- **The order:** one word, one gesture, and a room moves.
- **The arrival:** the car, the entourage, the door held open; we see the effect before the person.
- **The outsider's eyes:** the poor one, the new hire, the servant takes in the wealth, and it presses on them.
- **Careless excess:** something precious treated as nothing (a glass dropped, a cheque torn, a gift left unopened).
- **Two powers:** two families or two bosses sizing each other up through things: the car, the watch, who waits for whom.

## Staging and blocking
- Space is status: the bigger, emptier, higher room belongs to the powerful; the outsider is squeezed into doorways and corners.
- Service makes it visible: staff, drivers and assistants at the edges, doors opened before anyone reaches them.
- Who sits and who stands: the powerful sit while others stand, or keep others waiting.
- Status lives in a few specific things (the car, the watch, the shoes, the cut of a coat, the size of a desk). Pick two or three and keep them identical every time.
- The powerful one is still; everyone else moves.

## Performance
- The powerful never hurry and rarely raise their voice. Boredom is power.
- The outsider's body tightens: shoulders in, hands hidden, eyes taking it all in too fast.
- Staff are alive: a quick glance, a held breath, a practised blank face.

## The turn
- Status is used: someone is put in their place, a door closes, an offer arrives that can't be refused.
- Or it is punctured: the outsider doesn't bow, the order is ignored, the powerful one shows a crack.

## After the turn / the end
- One image of the hierarchy, now stated: who stands where at the end.
- End on the person it matters to, not the chandelier.

## Traps
- Status said in dialogue ("he's the richest man in the city") when the room could show it.
- Every scene pushed to luxury, so nothing stands out.
- A powerful character who shouts and rushes.
- Wealth as a montage of things with no person reacting to them.
- Signals that drift: the car, the house or the watch changing between scenes.
