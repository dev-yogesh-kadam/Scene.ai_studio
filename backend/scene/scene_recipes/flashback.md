# Flashback / memory
Use when the story shows the past: a memory triggered in the present, a narrated past moment, a repressed truth surfacing, a dream. Mix with `reveal` for a twist, `grief` for someone lost, `romance` for a first meeting, `backstory` for the history around it.

## What the scene is really about
- The past matters only for what it does to the present. Know why the hero remembers it now.
- Memory is subjective: it shows what the person felt, not a neutral record.
- A flashback is a scene with its own turn, not a run of images.

## Pick the engine
- **The trigger:** a present object, sound or word opens the past (the ring, a song, a name).
- **The match:** the same image then and now: a hand, a door, a place.
- **Fragments:** brief flashes that return and grow, each showing more than the last.
- **The full scene:** one complete past moment played straight, then back.
- **The other side:** a past moment we saw before, now from a side that shows the truth.
- **Interwoven:** past and present in the same place, told in turns, each commenting on the other.

## Staging and blocking
- Put the hero in it: the past as they lived it (as a child, a witness, the one left behind). Someone else's memory only when that person is telling it.
- Dress and age everyone for that time; the place as it was then.
- The place stays recognisable: the same house, the same tree, changed by the years.
- One moment, one action, one feeling.

## Performance
- In the past, people are as they were: lighter, younger, before the wound.
- In the present, the hero meets the memory in the body: a breath, eyes closing, a hand going to the object.

## The turn
- The moment in the past that mattered: the promise, the betrayal, the death, the first touch.
- Or the truth the memory exposes, which changes the present.

## After the turn / the end
- Back in the present, the hero acts differently because of it.

## Traps
- A flashback with no reason to happen now.
- The present-day hero dropped into the past.
- A cheap past look by habit: heavy blur, sepia, vignette.
- Too long, so the present stalls.
- Replaying what the audience already saw, unchanged.
- Returning to a different present moment than the one left.
