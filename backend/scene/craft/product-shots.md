# Product photography and hero shots

A hero shot makes the product look like the best version of itself, in the moment it is most desirable. Not the whole
catalogue — one angle, one light, one detail that sells.

## Decide three things first
1. **The one quality to sell:** freshness, craft, luxury, power, fun, texture. Everything in the frame serves it.
2. **The context:** clean studio (e-commerce, catalogue) or lifestyle (the product in use, in its world).
3. **The hero angle:** the angle that shows its shape best. Bottles and cans: straight on or slightly low. Food:
   45° for most dishes, top-down for flat ones (pizza, bowls, flat lays), eye level for stacked ones (burgers, cakes).
   Watches and jewellery: three-quarter, close.

## Light for products
- **Soft key from the side or above-behind:** shape and texture, gentle shadows (food, cosmetics).
- **Rim / backlight:** outlines glass and liquids, makes drinks glow; separates a dark product from a dark ground.
- **Gradient background:** a soft light falloff behind the product looks premium.
- **Controlled reflections:** long soft highlights along a bottle; no messy reflections of the room.
- Hard light only for a bold, graphic, high-energy look (sport drinks, tech).

## Making it desirable
- **Food and drink:** steam, condensation, a drip, a crumb, the moment of the pour, the pull of melted cheese; shallow
  depth of field; ingredients scattered with intention.
- **Cosmetics:** texture swatches, smooth gradients, water droplets, clean pastel or marble grounds.
- **Tech:** dark grounds, rim light, the screen glowing, hard geometric shadows.
- **Fashion and accessories:** fabric movement, a hand, a detail of the stitching.
- **Hands:** a hand holding or using the product makes it human, but hands are the models' weak point: keep the hand
  simple (holding, not fiddling), partly out of frame, or out of focus.

## Composition
- The product sharp, the brightest and highest-contrast thing in frame.
- Props tell the story (coffee beans around the cup, lemons beside the drink) but never compete; odd numbers, varied
  heights.
- Negative space on one side for the line of type or the logo that goes on later as a designed page.
- For e-commerce: centred, full product visible, plain ground, consistent angle across the range.

## Video hero shots
- A slow orbit or push-in on the product (3–5 s); a pour or a splash in slow motion; a reveal from a macro detail.
- The product turning on a slow turntable with a moving rim light.
- Keep the product's shape and label stable: use the user's product photo as a reference (a NeuralCore prop entry, or
  a shot that starts on that picture with a mode that keeps it) rather than describing it in words.

## Labels and logos
- Readable brand words drawn by a model come out wrong. Use the user's own product picture as the starting frame, or
  keep the label turned away or soft, and put the brand on the end card as a designed page.
- Never invent a brand's logo; never add a real brand the user did not give.

## Traps
- A product floating in a random fantasy scene with no reason.
- Too many props.
- A garbled label in plain view.
- Hands doing fine work (opening a tiny clasp, typing) in close-up.
