# Shot grammar and coverage

Shot size is how close the audience stands to the story. Change it with intent: closer means more feeling and less
context; wider means more context and less feeling. A scene told in one size is flat; a scene that changes size on
every cut for no reason is noise.

## The sizes and what they do
| Size | Studio value | What it says | Use for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Extreme wide | EWS | the world is bigger than the person | openings, endings, scale, loneliness |
| Wide | WS | where we are, who is here, the geography | establishing, action across space |
| Medium | MS | what people do, body language | most action, two people talking |
| Medium close | MCU | what they feel while they act | dialogue, reactions, social hooks |
| Close-up | CU | the feeling itself | the turn of a scene, the emotional beat |
| Extreme close | ECU | one detail matters enormously | eyes, a ring, the product's texture, the clock |
| Insert | ECU/CU of an object | the clue, the action detail | hands on a key, the phone screen, the pour |

## Classic coverage of a scene
1. **Establish** (WS/EWS): the place and the time of day, 1 shot.
2. **Orient** (MS): who is where, what they are doing.
3. **Engage** (MCU / OTS pairs): the exchange or the action.
4. **Peak** (CU / ECU): the moment the scene turns.
5. **Release** (WS or a held MS): the new situation, room to breathe.
Not every piece needs all five. A 15 s reel may be hook (MCU) → action (MS) → detail (ECU) → payoff (MCU) → card.

## Angles
- **Eye level:** neutral, honest; the default.
- **Low angle:** power, heroism, threat; the product as monument.
- **High angle:** vulnerability, overview, smallness.
- **Top / overhead:** pattern, food, hands at work, maps.
- **Over the shoulder (OTS):** relationship; we stand with one character facing another.
Change angle when power changes in the scene, not at random.

## Composition, quickly
- Rule of thirds: eyes on the upper third line; the horizon on a third, not the middle (unless symmetry is the point).
- Lead room: a person looking or moving right gets space on the right.
- One focal point per frame; light, contrast, focus and colour all point to it.
- Foreground layers (a doorway, leaves, a shoulder) give depth and the feel of a real camera.
- Negative space is a choice: emptiness around a figure says loneliness; a full frame says energy.

## Variety without chaos
- Never three cuts in a row at the same size and angle, unless the repetition is the idea (a ritual, a montage rhyme).
- Cut between sizes at least two steps apart (WS → MCU, not WS → MS) or the cut feels like a jump.
- A move in, then a still frame: alternate motion and rest so the moves mean something.
- In a montage, rhyme shapes across cuts (a round cup → a round sun) instead of varying everything.

## In the studio
- Fill `shot_size`, `angle`, `move` and `end_state` on every scene step; the studio turns them into words.
- Open a scene wide, or on the hook for social video; go closer for the action; end wider or on the detail that
  pays the story off.
- Keep one shot = one action in one place: a 5 s clip cannot cover a whole scene.
- For angles of a picture that exists, see `skills/camera-coverage.md`; for a full shot plan, `skills/director.md`.

## Traps
- Every shot a medium shot at eye level: the "AI slideshow" look.
- A close-up of a face with no reason to be close.
- Cutting from WS to a slightly tighter WS.
- An insert of something that does not matter to the story.
