# Colour palettes and grading moods

Colour is mood you can control across every shot. Decide the palette once for the whole piece, write it the same way in
every prompt, and give the eye one accent colour to follow. A piece with every colour at full saturation has no
palette at all.

## Building a palette
- **Base (60%)**: the dominant tone of the world (warm wood, concrete grey, deep night blue).
- **Secondary (30%)**: supports or contrasts the base (skin tones, foliage, cream walls).
- **Accent (10%)**: one colour reserved for what matters (the red scarf, the brand's yellow, the neon sign). Use it
  only on the subject, the product or the clue.
- Write it as words for every prompt: "muted warm palette of cream, walnut and soft amber, with a single red accent".

## Grading moods
| Look | Words | Feels | Use for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm natural | warm, soft contrast, gentle highlights | inviting, human | cafe, food, family, brand films |
| Teal and orange | warm skin against cool teal shadows | cinematic, blockbuster | action, travel, sport |
| Desaturated cool | muted, cool, low saturation | serious, sad, cold | drama, thriller, grief |
| Bleach bypass | low saturation, high contrast, silvery | gritty, war, tension | action, conflict |
| Pastel | soft, light, low contrast, pastel tones | playful, gentle, youthful | lifestyle, beauty, kids |
| High saturation pop | vivid primaries, clean whites | energetic, fun | social, comedy, products for young audiences |
| Monochrome | black and white, rich contrast | timeless, memory, art | flashbacks, fashion, serious statements |
| Film stock | soft grain, gentle halation, slightly lifted blacks | nostalgic, real | brand films, music videos, period |
| Night neon | magenta, cyan, deep blacks, wet reflections | urban, electric | music, nightlife, chases |

## Colour and meaning (use, do not lecture)
- Red: passion, danger, appetite. Yellow: warmth, optimism, attention. Blue: calm, trust, cold, night.
- Green: nature, health, envy, unease (sickly green for horror). Orange: energy, friendliness, food.
- Culture changes meaning: white is mourning in parts of India; red is celebration and weddings. Ask the brief, not a
  chart.

## Brand colours
- Use the brand's colours as the accent or the end card's ground, not as a filter over the whole film.
- Put them in the world naturally: a cup, a door, a jacket, the light of a sign. Never a colour cast that tints faces.
- For motion graphics the palette comes from the logo (the film tool's `logo` op gives it).

## Consistency across shots
- One grade sentence repeated in every prompt of the piece; the same film look preset if you use one.
- Keep skin tones natural under any grade; a grade that turns faces green or orange looks cheap.
- Day-to-night or a flashback may change the grade: that change is a storytelling cut, do it once and clearly.

## In the studio
- Put the palette and grade in LIGHT AND LOOK (or the model guide's look section).
- A style reference in NeuralCore (type "style") carries a look across every shot better than words.
- Presets (`preset_search`, film looks and grades) hold tested look words.

## Traps
- "Vibrant colourful cinematic": no palette.
- A different grade for every shot of one scene.
- The brand colour washed over everything.
- Accents everywhere, so the eye follows nothing.
