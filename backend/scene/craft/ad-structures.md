# Ad structures

An ad is an argument in pictures. Pick the structure first, then the shots; a beautiful film with no structure is a
mood board that sells nothing. Every structure below has one job for the viewer at the end: the **call to action**
(visit, order, book, download, remember the name).

## The five that cover most briefs

**1. Problem → solution (15–30 s).** Show a small, real, recognisable frustration; introduce the product as the turn;
show life after. Beats: problem (2–4 s, close on a face or the moment of annoyance) → the turn (the product enters,
ideally in a hand) → the payoff (relief, joy, the result) → brand and line.
Best for: services, apps, household products, anything that fixes something. Trap: a fake, exaggerated problem.

**2. Demo (6–20 s).** The product doing its one best thing, shown, not told. Macro detail, the action, the result.
Beats: hook on the most striking moment → the action in 2–3 shots of rising size → the result held → logo.
Best for: food and drink, gadgets, cosmetics, anything with a satisfying physical moment (pour, sizzle, click, fold).

**3. Testimonial / face (15–30 s).** A real-feeling person, eye-line just off camera, saying one true sentence; cut
to what they talk about. Beats: face + first line (the hook) → B-roll of the thing → face again with the emotional line
→ brand. Best for: trust: clinics, schools, local shops, services. **Studio note:** talking faces need the Talking Head
workflow and the user's own voice track; or keep the person silent and put the words on screen as designed type.

**4. Before / after (6–15 s).** One frame of before, a transition (a wipe, a match cut, a snap), one frame of after,
the same framing so the change is obvious. Best for: cleaning, beauty, renovation, fitness, editing apps. Keep the
camera, light and framing identical across the cut; only the subject changes.

**5. Brand film (30–90 s).** Not a product argument but a feeling: who the brand is for and what it believes. Beats:
a world (2–3 shots) → a person in it with a want → a small moment of meaning → the brand as the quiet signature. One
emotion, one motif, one line at the end. Best for: launches, anniversaries, a place with a story (a cafe since 1972).

## Choosing quickly
- One product, one benefit, short attention → **demo**.
- Something that fixes a pain → **problem → solution**.
- Trust is the barrier → **testimonial**.
- The change is visible → **before / after**.
- The brief says "feel", "story", "who we are" → **brand film**.

## Local business (cafe, restaurant, salon, gym)
- Sell the **moment**, not the menu: the first sip on a rainy morning, the table of friends at 9 pm, the steam.
- Place is a character: an exterior that says where it is, one signature detail (the brass bell, the chalk board).
- Real hands, real food, warm practical light. People in soft focus or from behind; one hero face at most.
- The call to action is specific: "Open 7 am, Bandra West", a card at the end as a designed page (render_html), never
  words drawn by the video model.

## Shots that every structure needs
- An opening that hooks (see `social-hooks`).
- A hero shot of the product or place (see `product-shots`).
- A human moment (a hand, a smile, a reaction) — usually the shot people remember.
- An end card: logo, line, call to action, 2–3 s, as a page you design.

## Traps
- Five benefits in 15 seconds: pick one.
- The logo in the first second.
- Copying a famous ad's idea or characters: learn the structure, never the expression.
- Claims the user did not give ("best coffee in Mumbai", "100% organic"): never invent them.
