# Genre conventions

A genre is a promise to the audience about how it will feel. Keep the promise with the genre's grammar (light, camera,
pace, sound), then make one fresh choice so it is yours. For each kind of scene the studio also keeps deep craft in
`scene_presets/` (staging, engines, camera and cutting, traps): read the one that fits with `preset_search`.

## Drama
- **Feels:** truth, weight, people under pressure.
- **Light:** motivated, natural, often low key; window light and practicals.
- **Camera:** mostly static or slow push-ins; close-ups for the turn; let reactions play.
- **Pace:** unhurried; holds; silence matters.
- **Fresh choice:** one striking visual metaphor (an empty chair, a stopped clock), used twice.
- **Presets:** `confrontation`, `grief`, `conversation`, `reveal`.

## Comedy
- **Feels:** surprise, timing, warmth.
- **Light:** bright, high key, clean; nothing hidden in shadow.
- **Camera:** wider than you think (comedy is in bodies and space), static to let the gag play; a snap cut or a whip
  for the punch.
- **Pace:** setup — pause — punch. The pause is the joke. Cut right after the laugh.
- **Fresh choice:** a straight-faced performance in an absurd situation.
- **Presets:** `comedy`. **Model note:** physical gags with precise contact (a pie in a face) often fail; stage the
  before and the after, and cut on the moment.

## Horror
- **Feels:** dread, then shock.
- **Light:** low key, a single source, darkness the eye must search; cold or sickly colour.
- **Camera:** slow pushes toward doorways; static wides with something wrong in the background; no music sting
  without a reason.
- **Pace:** slow build, long holds, a sudden cut. Show less: the unseen frightens more.
- **Fresh choice:** horror in daylight, or in an ordinary place (a kitchen, a school corridor).
- **Presets:** `horror`, `suspense`. **Model note:** monsters in full view look fake; use shadows, silhouettes, and
  reactions.

## Action
- **Feels:** momentum, stakes, clarity.
- **Light:** high contrast, often teal and orange, practical sparks and fire.
- **Camera:** tracking and handheld, low angles for heroes; but geography first: a wide to say where everyone is.
- **Pace:** accelerating cuts; one clear action per shot; a beat of stillness before the biggest hit.
- **Fresh choice:** an action scene told from a bystander's or a child's view.
- **Presets:** `chase`, `fight`, `triumph`. **Model note:** fast complex contact (punches landing, cars colliding)
  fails; split into simple shots: wind-up, reaction, aftermath.

## Romance
- **Feels:** longing, closeness, warmth.
- **Light:** warm backlight, golden hour, soft haze, practical candles and fairy lights.
- **Camera:** slow, close, shallow focus; two-shots that bring them nearer over the piece; orbit for the kiss.
- **Pace:** lingering; looks held a beat longer than comfortable.
- **Fresh choice:** a romance told only through hands, or through one object passed between them.
- **Presets:** `romance`.

## Documentary
- **Feels:** truth, observation, intimacy with real life.
- **Light:** available light; no glamour.
- **Camera:** handheld, eye level, reframing as life happens; interviews framed off-centre, eyeline just off lens.
- **Pace:** follows life; B-roll of hands, places and details over the voice.
- **Fresh choice:** a single long observational shot where most would cut.
- **Studio note:** for a real person's own testimony use the user's voice track and the talking-head workflow; never
  invent a real person's words.

## Mixing genres
Most good pieces mix: a romantic comedy, a horror ad, an action-comedy chase. Pick the genre that sets the look and
pace, and the one that sets the tone of the ending. Name both in the concept.
